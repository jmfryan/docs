**Pikachu** (Japanese: ピカチュウ _Pikachū_) is the Electric Mouse Pokémon and the most recognisable face of the entire Pokémon franchise. Designated **#025** in the National Pokédex, Pikachu was introduced in Generation I and has appeared in every mainline game, anime series, and film since.

## Origin and design

Pikachu was designed by **Atsuko Nishida** during the development of _Pocket Monsters Red and Green_, which launched in Japan on 27 February 1996. Nishida has said in interviews that the early concept drew from squirrels and from _daifuku_ (Japanese rice cakes) — the round cheeks and small body silhouette were a deliberate echo of that imagery. Ken Sugimori later refined Nishida's drafts into the more streamlined design used in the games' final art.

<Note>
  **Originally a Fighting/Electric dual-type.** It was rumoured in the very earliest design documents, Pikachu was sketched as a Fighting/Electric Pokémon, before the team simplified the typing to pure Electric to make it more approachable for new players. The dual-typing only ever existed on paper and never made it into a shipped game. However, This was never true!
</Note>

The name is a portmanteau of two Japanese onomatopoeia: **_pika_**, the sound of a sparking electrical discharge, and **_chū_**, the squeak of a mouse. Together they describe Pikachu in a single word — a small, squeaking creature that crackles with electricity.

## Evolution line

Pikachu sits in the middle of a three-stage evolutionary line:

Many trainers — including Ash Ketchum in the anime — choose never to evolve their Pikachu, preferring its speed, charm, and signature attacks over Raichu's raw power.

## Combat profile

Pikachu's small size belies a competitive moveset built around fast, precise electric strikes:

- **Thunderbolt** — the iconic signature move, a reliable special Electric attack.
- **Quick Attack** — a priority physical move that lets Pikachu strike first.
- **Iron Tail** — a Steel-type physical attack covering Rock and Ice matchups.
- **Volt Tackle** — Pikachu's exclusive signature move (originally an egg move from a Pichu hatched while the parent held a Light Ball). High damage with recoil.
- **Thunder** — a high-power Electric attack with reduced accuracy in clear weather.

Held alongside a **Light Ball**, Pikachu's Attack and Special Attack are doubled, pushing it surprisingly close to fully-evolved Pokémon in offensive output.

## In the anime

In the _Pokémon_ anime, Pikachu has been the partner of protagonist **Ash Ketchum** since the very first episode of _Pokémon: Indigo League_ (1997). Ash's Pikachu refuses to live in a Poké Ball, travelling on Ash's shoulder instead — a quirk that became one of the franchise's defining visual motifs.

The character has been voiced by **Ikue Ohtani** in Japanese (and most international dubs) since 1997 — one of the longest continuous voice-acting roles in television history. Pikachu's vocabulary in the anime is famously limited to variations of its own name, but Ohtani's performance carries a remarkable range of emotion through tone alone.

## Cultural footprint

- **Mascot of the franchise.** Pikachu has appeared on the box art and marketing of nearly every Pokémon spin-off, including _Pokémon Yellow_ (1998), _Pokémon Snap_ (1999), _Pokémon Café Mix_, and _Detective Pikachu_.
- **Detective Pikachu (2019).** A live-action film starring Ryan Reynolds as the voice of a coffee-loving Detective Pikachu, becoming the highest-grossing video-game film at the time of its release.
- **Times Square balloon.** A Pikachu balloon has appeared in the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade nearly every year since 2001.
- **Smash Bros.** A playable character in every entry of _Super Smash Bros._ since the original 1999 title.

## Where to find Pikachu

In the mainline games, Pikachu appears most reliably in:

- The **Viridian Forest** (Kanto, _Red/Blue/Yellow_ and remakes)
- The **Power Plant** (Kanto, _FireRed/LeafGreen_)
- **Route 2** in Galar (_Sword/Shield_)
- **South Province (Area One)** in Paldea (_Scarlet/Violet_)

In _Pokémon Yellow_, Pikachu is given to the player as their starter — the only mainline game where Pikachu is unavoidable, and the inspiration for _Pokémon: Let's Go, Pikachu!_ (2018).
